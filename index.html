Why The Equal Lens

Gender inequality is not a sudden crisis. It is a gradual construction. It begins in early childhood — in language, in expectations, in subtle reinforcement of roles.

Globally:

  • 1 in 3 women experience physical or sexual violence in their lifetime (WHO).
  • Over 200 million girls and women have undergone female genital mutilation (UNICEF).
  • Women hold only 26% of parliamentary seats worldwide (UN Women).
  • In India, nearly 30% of women report domestic violence (NFHS-5).
  • Girls are significantly underrepresented in STEM leadership fields.

These statistics are not isolated incidents. They reflect systemic patterns that begin forming in childhood.

Children absorb gender norms before they consciously understand them. Through toys, language, media, classrooms, and family roles — expectations are silently embedded.

We believe gender equality should not be taught through passive lectures alone.

Interactive learning — through games, dialogue, and reflection — has deeper psychological impact.

When children notice patterns themselves, they build critical thinking. When they build critical thinking, they develop agency.

The Equal Lens exists to intervene at the beginning — where inequality first takes shape.
